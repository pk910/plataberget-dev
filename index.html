Platåberget is the public testnet for Gloas / the Glamsterdam upgrade (enshrined proposer-builder separation, block-level access lists, gas repricing and more). Use it to test client software, staking setups, builders, and infrastructure against the new fork — it is expected to stay alive until Glamsterdam ships on mainnet. FAQ

How do I deposit a validator?

Generate your keys with ethstaker-deposit-cli. Platåberget is in its built-in network list from `v1.3.1` 

./deposit new-mnemonic \
    --compounding --amount 32 \
    --withdrawal_address 0xYourWithdrawalAddress \
    --chain plataberget
                        

                            --compounding gives the validator 0x02 credentials (balances up to 2048 ETH).
                            Grab at least 32 test ETH from the
                            faucet, then submit the
                            generated deposit_data-*.json on Dora:
                            Validators → Submit Deposits
                            (connect wallet, upload file, send). Never enter a mainnet mnemonic or keystore into any website.
How do I become a builder? (pre- & post-Gloas)

Under ePBS (EIP-7732), builders are a separate on-chain actor with their own registry. A builder is identified by withdrawal credentials starting with the 0xB0 prefix (not to be confused with 0x0B, the builder domain type). There are two onboarding paths:

Before the Gloas fork (epoch 1,536 — Aug 20, 07:50 UTC): submit a deposit with 0xB0 withdrawal credentials through the regular validator deposit contract. Deposits that are still waiting in the pending-deposit queue when Gloas activates are onboarded as builders at the fork. Timing is critical: a 0xB0 deposit that gets processed before the fork becomes a regular validator instead. The Dora Builder Deposits page tracks every 0xB0 deposit and projects its fate at the fork — check it before you send anything. ethstaker-deposit-cli `v1.3.1` or later can produce 0xB0 credentials using its `./deposit builder` command. Do not generate 0xB0 validator keys, use it for builders only

After the Gloas fork: builders join through the dedicated EIP-8282 builder deposit contract (0x0000bff4…300d8282, already deployed). Go to Dora → Builders → Submit Deposit, connect a wallet and upload builder deposit data (pubkey, 0xB0 withdrawal credentials, amount, and a proof-of-possession signed under the dedicated builder-deposit domain) — or use the built-in generator, which is fine for this devnet.

Is it safe to deposit a builder yet?

Not yet (as of network launch): the pending-deposit queue is short, so a 0xB0 deposit sent now would be processed within hours — long before Gloas activates — and would end up as a regular validator, not a builder. The Builder Deposits page shows the live projection ("N new builders projected to be onboarded at the fork") and warns while the queue is too short. Wait until the projection flips, closer to the fork, before depositing. Once Gloas is active, use the builder deposit contract instead — that path has no timing caveat.

How do the deposit queues work?

Validator deposits (pre- and post-Gloas) go through the Electra-style pending-deposit queue: deposits are queued in order and processed subject to the activation churn limit (256 ETH per epoch on this network). Dora shows the current queue length and estimated wait on its deposits pages.

Builder deposits (post-Gloas, EIP-8282) use an EIP-7002-style fee market instead: the contract targets 8 deposits per block, and the request fee starts at 1 wei and ramps up exponentially while the backlog stays above target (it is currently at the 1 wei floor). The consensus layer consumes at most 64 builder deposit requests per payload. On the way out, builder exits are capped at 16 per payload, an exiting builder becomes withdrawable after 64 epochs, and the withdrawal sweep scans up to 16,384 builders per block, sharing the withdrawal pipeline with the validator sweep.

Amsterdam Gas Repricings

Glamsterdam reprices gas around the real cost of state: a new per-state-byte dimension makes state creation several times more expensive, while plain transfers get cheaper. Key changes:

What to watch out for:

  • State-heavy operations are the big one: anything creating new accounts or storage slots (minting, airdrops, factories, first-time token holders) costs roughly 4–9× more. Re-benchmark those flows here before Glamsterdam reaches mainnet.
  • Never rely on hardcoded gas amounts — fixed gas for subcalls, cached estimates, and tx-cost calculators all need retesting. Wallet and bundler intrinsic-gas math changes too (base 12,000 + 6,000 for value transfers).
  • Plain ETH transfers get cheaper (18,000 vs 21,000).
  • Refund behavior changes (EIP-7778 block gas accounting without refunds, storage-clear refund 11,616) — recheck refund-dependent patterns.
  • Calldata floor cost rises to 16 per token (EIP-7976) — data-heavy transactions pay more.
  • Access lists are repriced (EIP-7981) — re-benchmark before relying on them for savings.
  • The block gas limit ramps to 200M at epoch 1,566, right after the fork — plenty of headroom for load testing.
